MCA-Bench: A Multimodal Benchmark for Evaluating CAPTCHA Robustness Against VLM-based Attacks

Zonglin Wu, Yule Xue, Yaoyao Feng, Xiaolong Wang, Yiren Song*

published at AAAI 2026 · Oral Presentation

paper
Abstract +

As automated attack techniques rapidly advance, CAPTCHAs remain a critical defense mechanism against malicious bots. However, existing CAPTCHA schemes encompass a diverse range of modalities—from static distorted text and obfuscated images to interactive clicks, sliding puzzles, and logic-based questions—yet the community still lacks a unified, large-scale, multimodal benchmark to rigorously evaluate their security robustness. To address this gap, we introduce MCA-Bench, a comprehensive and reproducible benchmarking suite that integrates heterogeneous CAPTCHA types into a single evaluation protocol. Leveraging a shared vision–language model backbone, we fine-tune specialized cracking agents for each CAPTCHA category, enabling consistent, cross-modal assessments. Extensive experiments reveal that MCA-Bench effectively maps the vulnerability spectrum of modern CAPTCHA designs under varied attack settings, and—crucially—offers the first quantitative analysis of how challenge complexity, interaction depth, and model solvability interrelate. Based on these findings, we propose three actionable design principles and identify key open challenges, laying the groundwork for systematic CAPTCHA hardening, fair benchmarking, and broader community collaboration.

AnatoMaskGAN: GNN-driven Slice Feature Fusion and Noise Augmentation for Medical Semantic Image Synthesis

Zonglin Wu, Yule Xue, Qianxiang Hu, Yaoyao Feng, Yuqi Ma, Shanxiong Chen*

published at BIBM 2025

paper
Abstract +

Medical semantic-mask synthesis enhances data augmentation and analysis, yet most GAN-based methods still generate one-to-one images without maintaining spatial consistency across slices. To overcome this, we propose AnatoMaskGAN, a novel framework that embeds interslice spatial features, integrates diverse augmentation strategies, and optimizes deep feature learning for complex medical scans. Specifically, a GNN-based slice-feature fusion module models spatial relationships and aggregates contextual cues from neighboring slices, capturing anatomical structures more comprehensively. A 3D spatial noise-injection strategy fuses weighted spatial features with noise to enrich structural diversity, while a grayscale-texture classifier refines grayscale and texture representation during generation. Experiments on L2R-OASIS and L2R-Abdomen CT show that AnatoMaskGAN achieves 26.50 dB PSNR and 0.9229 SSIM on L2R-OASIS, surpassing the state of the art by 0.43 dB, and reaches 0.8602 SSIM on L2R-Abdomen CT, a 0.48-point improvement. Ablation studies confirm that removing any of the three modules degrades PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, verifying the effectiveness of each component in enhancing reconstruction fidelity and perceptual quality. Code is available at https://github.com/noheadwuzonglin/AnatoMaskGAN.

A Dual-Branch Topology-Aware Model for Enhanced 3D Vascular Segmentation and Multi-Center Adaptation

Yuqi Ma, Shanxiong Chen, Hailing Xiong, WenSong Yang, Xiaolong Wang, Zonglin Wu, Cheng Huang, Xin Guan

published in Knowledge-Based Systems (Elsevier), 2026

paper
Abstract +

Three-dimensional (3D) vascular segmentation is essential for cerebrovascular disease diagnosis, surgical planning, and quantitative vascular analysis. However, accurate segmentation from Magnetic Resonance Angiography (MRA) remains challenging due to complex vascular topology, thin and tortuous branches, pathological morphological variations, and domain shifts caused by different imaging centers. Existing deep learning methods often focus on local voxel-wise representation and may fail to preserve global vascular connectivity or maintain stable performance across centers. To address these challenges, we propose a Dual-Branch Topology-Aware Model (DBT) for 3D vascular segmentation and multi-center adaptation. The proposed framework introduces a vascular topology-aware network to model long-range structural dependencies of vascular trees, and a dual-branch multi-frequency adaptive fusion network to jointly enhance global vessel structures and fine-branch details. Furthermore, a multi-center domain adaptation module is incorporated to learn domain-invariant representations and improve cross-center generalization. Extensive experiments on four cerebrovascular datasets, including ADAM, HH, Loch, and TubeTK, demonstrate that DBT-Base, which excludes the multi-center domain adaptation module, and the complete DBT model achieve competitive segmentation performance compared with representative 3D medical image segmentation methods under single-center and multi-center settings, respectively. Quantitative and qualitative results show that DBT improves vessel continuity, fine-branch delineation, and robustness to unseen-center data. Ablation studies further validate the contribution of each component. These findings indicate that DBT provides an effective and generalizable framework for multi-center 3D vascular segmentation. Code is available at: https://github.com/ma-yu-qi/DBT.

FCSG-Net: Frequency-domain Crossscale Gated Network for Stroke Segmentation

Yaoyao Feng, Peng Xie*, Shanxiong Chen, Yongjun Zhu, Xiaolong Wang, Zonglin Wu, Gaoshan Song

published in Biomedical Signal Processing and Control

paper
Abstract +

Accurate segmentation of stroke lesions is vital for treatment planning and prognosis evaluation. However, current deep learning-based methods still face bottlenecks in handling irregular and scattered small lesions, including limited long-range dependency modeling, loss of local details, and inadequate cross-scale fusion. Moreover, pure Transformer architectures incur high computational costs, hindering real-time clinical application. Manual delineation is also labor-intensive and subject to inter-observer variability, affecting algorithm training. To address these challenges, we propose a Frequency-domain Cross-scale Gated Network (FCSG-Net) that integrates the strengths of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Transformers. Specifically, a Linear Aggregation Attention mechanism enables efficient global modeling while reducing attention complexity. Although the overall FLOPs of FCSG-Net are slightly higher than some lightweight models (e.g., 30G for TransUNet), they remain around 40G, achieving a favorable balance between accuracy and efficiency. The proposed Frequency-enhanced Dual Pooling Feature Enhancement (FDPFE) and Cross-scale Gated Fusion (CS-Fusion) modules dynamically optimize local details and global semantics. We systematically evaluated FCSG-Net on multimodal MRI stroke datasets (T1W, FLAIR, DWI, ADC) from ATLAS and ISLES22. Results show that FCSG-Net achieves average Dice scores of 67.94% and 72.84%, and Precision of 74.37% and 80.96% on ISLES22 and ATLAS, respectively, while maintaining high accuracy and efficiency. FCSG-Net can assist clinicians in rapidly and accurately identifying lesion boundaries, particularly for small lesions, thus providing reliable support for early stroke diagnosis and personalized treatment.

MSDFNET: An Aggregation Perception-based Multi-modal Skin Lesion Classification Network

Xiaolong Wang, Mingyue Yang, Yaoyao Feng, Zonglin Wu, Shanxiong Chen

published at ICASSP 2026

paper
Abstract +

Accurate classification of multimodal skin diseases is vital for improving patient prognosis. However, the heterogeneity of image modalities and simplicity and lack of dynamism in existing fusion methods affect diagnostic effectiveness. This paper proposes an aggregate perception-based multimodal skin lesion classification model with a three-stage progressive fusion framework: in the dual-modal interaction stage, cross-modal complementarity between dermoscopic and clinical images is achieved through collaborative feature learning; in the aggregate perception stage, dynamic fusion of multi-stage image features generates more discriminative representations; the metadata fusion module integrates clinical text and image features with dual self-attention. Experiments demonstrate that the model performs excellently in core diagnostic and all tasks, significantly outperforming advanced models and providing reliable support for clinical diagnosis.

G^2RA-NET: Graph-based Cross-Slice Relation Modeling with Attention Gating for Medical Image Segmentation

Shengye Wang, Zonglin Wu*, Liang Fan, Yule Xue, Haozhe Zhao

submitted to ICASSP 2027

Abstract +

Medical image segmentation supports quantitative clinical analysis and computer-aided diagnosis. Recent methods for medical image segmentation have improved both local feature representation and volumetric context modeling. However, existing methods still strug- gle to efficiently model cross-slice relations in anisotropic volumet- ric images, limiting segmentation consistency and accuracy. This pa- per proposes G^2RA-Net, a medical image segmentation framework that combines graph-based cross-slice relation modeling with atten- tion gating. Graph-Based Slice Relationship Modeling (GSRM) cap- tures anatomical dependencies across consecutive slices by repre- senting each slice as a graph node and propagating semantic con- text through graph message passing. The Cross-Slice Attention Gate (CSAG) then selects relevant neighboring context and emphasizes target anatomical regions through attention-guided feature modula- tion. Experiments on brain MRI and abdominal CT datasets demon- strate that G^2RA-Net outperforms representative methods in seg- mentation accuracy and boundary quality. Ablation studies further validate the proposed design.
